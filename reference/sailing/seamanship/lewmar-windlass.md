## Original summary

A powered windlass takes time to deploy and recover an anchor. Its published maximum line speed is an equipment specification, not a guaranteed rate under load. Lewmar instructs the operator to ease the boat toward the anchor during recovery and to avoid using the windlass to pull the boat or force a stalled recovery. Pay out rode with controlled sternway so it does not pile onto the anchor; control the final approach to the bow carefully. At anchor and when stowed, secure the load independently of the windlass.

## Verified sections

- Section 9.6, page 37, lists maximum line speeds of 28 m/min for the V1 12 V 700 W, 29 m/min for the V2 12 V 700 W and 24 V 900 W, and 39 m/min for the V3 12 V and 24 V 1000 W. These are maximum specifications for those configurations, not measured loaded deployment or recovery speeds.
- Section 7.1, page 17, describes manually controlled braked free-fall, which differs from powered lowering.
- Section 7.2, page 18, distinguishes DOWN and UP operations, release of anchor locks before deployment, and independent securing after recovery.
- Section 7.5, page 21, covers boat movement during deployment and recovery, avoiding overload and stall, careful handling near the bow, and transferring anchoring loads to a chain stopper or independent strong point.
- Section 2.1, page 3, covers keeping people and loose clothing clear of moving equipment, checking for swimmers and divers, independent securing, isolation, and retaining breaker protection.

## Application and limits

The fictional simulator uses powered lowering at 24 m/min (0.4 m/s) and retrieval at 15 m/min (0.25 m/s). These are explicit project assumptions below the cited V1–V3 maximum line speeds; Lewmar does not validate either simulated rate. The model separates selected target length from actual paid-out rode and requires vessel movement to unload a taut rode during recovery. Stopping the windlass stops rode travel, not vessel motion.

The simulator does not reproduce a particular Lewmar windlass, electrical supply, motor temperature, breaker, clutch, free-fall operation, crew handling, seabed breakout force, or loaded performance curve. A geometric release near the anchor is not a simulation of physically breaking it out. Reaching a target length, reaching the seabed, or completing simulated recovery does not demonstrate real anchor setting, safe scope, safe handling, or independent securing. Select real scope and procedures from the actual vessel, equipment manuals, conditions, and qualified instruction.
